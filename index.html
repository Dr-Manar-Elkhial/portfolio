Profile

About

Fifteen years moving between the bench, the laboratory and the lecture theatre.

I am a heritage scientist and objects conservator specialising in the long-term care of organic heritage materials — archaeological wood, papyrus, parchment, textiles and painted surfaces. My work sits at the intersection of hands-on conservation treatment, environmental risk management, and analytical research, informed by training in pXRF, FTIR, SEM-EDX, multispectral imaging and ND-IR spectroscopy.

For over a decade I led the Preventive Conservation Unit at the Grand Egyptian Museum, where I built environmental monitoring programmes, directed Integrated Pest Management and modified-atmosphere treatment for organic collections, and engineered Egypt's first inert-atmosphere fumigation system. I now hold a joint role as Assistant Professor of Heritage Science and Director of the International Collaboration Office at Ain Shams University, where I teach preventive conservation and heritage science, supervise postgraduate research, and coordinate international partnerships with institutions in Germany, the United States and beyond.

My PhD (Cairo University, First-Class Honours) examined the optimisation of modified inert-atmosphere fumigation for insect disinfestation — work that continues to shape how I think about evidence-based, minimally interventive conservation decisions in both museum and teaching contexts.

Research & analytical work

My research translates laboratory analysis into practical conservation decisions — from oxygen-barrier testing for modified-atmosphere systems to non-dispersive infrared detection of active insect infestation. Selected instrumentation and methods:

pXRFFTIR (ATR/transmission)SEM-EDX Multispectral imagingND-IR spectroscopy X-ray radiographyColourimetryMicroscopy

This work has coordinated analytical assessment for 70+ complex organic objects at the Grand Egyptian Museum, and underpins ongoing collaborations including the Ta'ziz Science Cooperation Project (Ain Shams University, University of Marburg, AUB) and the El-Hiba Coffins Conservation Project (Fayoum University & DAI Cairo).
