Archaeological Wood & Polychrome Objects

Materials: Wood, pigment, binding media · Role: Lead conservator & analyst · Context: Museum collection, New Kingdom material

Add photo: polychrome coffin, analysis or treatment in progress

Condition & documentation

Polychrome wooden coffins present layered conservation challenges: a wooden substrate, painted surfaces, structural condition and environmental vulnerability all interacting. Documentation captured the relationship between substrate and polychromy before any treatment decision was made.

Analysis & investigation

Multispectral imaging, FTIR and microscopy revealed hidden information about pigment and binder composition and identified deterioration mechanisms — this analytical stage directly shaped the treatment plan, rather than confirming a decision already made.

Treatment

Treatment integrated analytical evidence with practical, minimally interventive decisions addressing structural and surface deterioration, always weighing intervention against long-term material stability.

Outcome & professional judgement

This case exemplifies evidence-based conservation judgement under complexity: where wood science, pigment chemistry and structural risk had to be reasoned through together rather than treated as separate problems.
